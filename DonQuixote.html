    § 01 · ON THE RECORD

    A protest that happens to sing

    When Jim Breen walked into Contra Costa Superior Court without a lawyer, he carried a binder of statutes, a baritone he didn't know he had, and one fixed idea: his daughter Clara deserved her father in the room.

    He won fair, equal access — and learned how heavy the door swings shut behind you. The Sons of Quixote is that door, held open. A chorus of 20 to 25 pro per fathers trading war stories, court forms, and harmony. Proof, sung in four parts, that a man can stand for himself and still never stand alone.

    We are not a law firm. We are not a grievance machine. We are a rehearsal that spills into the hallway outside Dept. 22, a documentary camera pointed at a system that too often mistakes silence for consent, and a supply table that funds the whole impossible enterprise.

    CH. II · MOVEMENT 02

    The Impossible Dream

    A feature-length documentary · in production. Twenty-five fathers, one song from Man of La Mancha, and a camera pointed at a system that too often mistakes silence for consent.

    The film follows the chorus from its first uncertain rehearsal to the night twenty-five pro per fathers sing “The Impossible Dream” in public — and intercuts that crescendo with the dockets, hallways, and waiting rooms where the real score is written.

    Nothing here is reenacted that can be reclaimed. Testimony is read on camera by the man who lived it. Court documents appear as themselves. The rehearsal audio carries the film the way a score carries a scene — because that is what it is.

    CH. III · MOVEMENT 03

    The Director

    One father's docket became a movement's storyboard. The man holding the camera has stood exactly where the camera points.

    Jim Breen

    Founder · Director · Pro Per Father

    Jim Breen represented himself in Contra Costa Superior Court and won fair, equal access to his daughter Clara. The cost of that win was an education nobody should have to self-fund: civil procedure at midnight, evidence rules at lunch, appellate timelines on the backs of rehearsal sheets.

    With documentary credits on file and a hard drive full of testimony reels, Jim turned the lens on the hallway he'd paced for years — and found twenty-odd fathers pacing it with him. The Sons of Quixote is his second production: the first was his own case.

    “I didn't start a choir because I love to sing. I started one because twenty-five fathers singing is harder to ignore than one man with a binder.”
    — Jim Breen, Dept. 22 hallway, 07:40
