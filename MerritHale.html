Notes from the foundry

Weather, carried indoors

These paintings begin as weather. A season of low light on the New Hampshire coast, a field gone the colour of raw umber in November — carried into the studio and worked until the colour stops describing and starts standing still. The Meridian Series is the slowest work I've done: six canvases, three years, one horizon.

Each piece is photographed at 600 DPI and hung here at true proportion, so a 96-inch field reads on your screen the way it will read above your sideboard. Nothing is flattened, nothing is flattered.

— M.H.

The Studio · Hampstead, NH

A converted foundry, twelve-foot walls, one long horizon.

Merritt Hale paints the weather of southern New Hampshire the way it feels to stand inside it — unhurried, a little austere, and suddenly generous with light.

The studio sits at the end of Foundry Lane in a building that poured iron for a century before it poured light. Hale bought it in 2016 for the north wall — forty feet of it, unbroken — and has been answering that wall ever since.

The work is slow on purpose. Pigments are ground and tested against the season they're meant to hold: cadmium red medium for the August horizon, ultramarine for the ten minutes of dusk that the Tide Studies chase. Nothing leaves the easel until it can stand beside the weather that made it.

"A painting is finished when the colour stops describing the field and starts standing still in it."
— Merritt Hale, studio notebook, 2023

Visitors are received Tuesday through Saturday, by appointment. The Moving Wall runs continuously, works come out of storage on request, and no one is ever asked to buy.

Asked often

Answered plainly.

Yes. Every original travels in a custom-built crate with climate buffering, insured door-to-door, with white-glove placement available in most major cities. A 96-inch canvas has crossed the Atlantic twice without a single complaint.

Each available work is offered in three sizes — 24, 36 and 48 inches on the longest side — in editions of fifty, printed from the 600 DPI captures, signed and numbered by the artist. The "At Scale" stage in each viewing room shows exactly how a size will sit above furniture.

A small number each year, typically eight to twelve weeks from first conversation to varnishing day. Commissions begin with a studio visit or a long phone call about the wall the work will live on — light, furniture, the weather outside the nearest window.

Ninety minutes, the Moving Wall running, works pulled from storage on request, and a kettle that's always on. You'll never be asked to buy, and you're welcome to photograph anything except the artist mid-sentence.
